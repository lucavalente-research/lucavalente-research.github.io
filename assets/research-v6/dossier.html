Liquidity under the surface

The archived macro review recorded a $1.191 billion contraction in combined USDT and USDC supply over seven days. That is a liquidity warning, but it does not establish that every protocol faced the same funding conditions or the same selling pressure.

A useful reading combines money entering the ecosystem, trading depth and the ownership structure of each token. These are different observations: a large exchange wallet may represent many customers, and locked supply may not be available for immediate sale.

Concentration is not executable liquidity

The CRV, UNI and OP ownership review shows why a holder ranking requires context. Custody, exchange balances, locked holdings and governance participation are not interchangeable. A concentrated distribution can coexist with liquid trading, while a dispersed holder base does not guarantee depth during stress.

Reading the protocol universe

The terminal covers 199 protocols. Its market pulse is a normalized archive composite, not a sum of investable capital or token cash distributions. 177 protocols have at least one revenue or TVL history; 22 use flat archived-snapshot proxies. Incomplete map observations use disclosed activity, size or resilience proxies. Those outputs are comparative research aids, not measured cash flows or calibrated risk probabilities.

What would confirm improvement?

Look for persistent stabilization in stablecoin supply, broader participation in trading activity and improving protocol revenue. Reassess concentration with wallet context and verify fresh observations before making a decision. A single improving series cannot settle the wider liquidity question.
